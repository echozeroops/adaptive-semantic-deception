# Semantic Model

The ASD semantic model defines how meaning, perception, artifacts, and observer-state evolution are represented inside the architecture.

ASD treats interaction as a semantic process rather than a simple exchange of messages.

---

## Core Assumption

Every observer interaction modifies meaning.

An emitted artifact is not only an output.

It becomes part of:
- ProjectionWorld history
- observer interpretation
- future consistency requirements
- hypothesis reconstruction
- defensive pacing

ASD therefore treats semantic state as persistent.

---

## Semantic State

Semantic State represents the current meaning-space of the projected environment.

It may include:
- visible structures
- artifact relationships
- naming continuity
- perceived operational logic
- historical traces
- ambiguity level
- observer confidence
- suspicion indicators
- exposure depth

Semantic State is not identical to system state.

It represents what the observer can infer.

---

## Observer-Relative Meaning

ASD assumes that meaning is observer-relative.

The same artifact may produce different interpretations depending on:
- prior interaction history
- current ReconstructedHypothesis
- observer confidence
- exploration strategy
- suspicion level
- perceived context

This is why ASD models the observer as a cognitive process rather than a passive receiver.

---

## Artifact Meaning

Artifacts are semantic carriers.

An artifact may influence:
- perceived structure
- confidence
- uncertainty
- exploration direction
- hypothesis stability
- suspicion accumulation

Artifacts must be evaluated not only by content, but by their effect on observer interpretation.

---

## Semantic Continuity

Semantic Continuity is the requirement that meaning remains coherent across time.

Continuity applies to:
- names
- relationships
- timelines
- topology
- metadata
- interaction history
- apparent operational behavior

Failure of semantic continuity can destabilize ProjectionWorld.

---

## Semantic Drift

Semantic Drift occurs when ProjectionWorld meaning changes in uncontrolled or inconsistent ways.

Possible causes include:
- excessive adaptation
- contradictory artifacts
- incoherent timing
- observer-model mismatch
- uncontrolled AI generation
- weak consistency validation

Semantic Drift increases suspicion and reduces defensive stability.

---

## Ambiguity

ASD does not attempt to remove all ambiguity.

Controlled ambiguity is useful because it:
- slows confident interpretation
- preserves uncertainty
- prevents premature closure
- supports defensive time acquisition
- reduces brittle projection behavior

Ambiguity must remain bounded and coherent.

---

## Semantic Pressure

Observer interaction applies semantic pressure to ProjectionWorld.

This pressure may include:
- consistency probing
- rapid exploration
- contradiction testing
- boundary testing
- timing analysis
- artifact correlation

ASD must maintain semantic stability under this pressure.

---

## Projection Meaning Layer

ProjectionWorld exists as a meaning layer between:
- the defensive root environment
- the observer cognitive surface

```text
Defensive Root Environment
        ↓
Sanitized Semantic Constraints
        ↓
ProjectionWorld
        ↓
Artifacts
        ↓
Observer Interpretation
```

The observer never receives raw truth.

The observer receives controlled semantic projection.

---

## Semantic Control Goals

The ASD semantic model attempts to maintain:

- coherent projection meaning
- bounded uncertainty
- controlled artifact interpretation
- stable observer-state evolution
- defensive time acquisition
- safety-bounded adaptation
- explainable semantic decisions
