# Threat Model

The ASD architecture assumes that observers actively attempt to:
- validate consistency
- detect artificiality
- identify hidden structure
- accelerate discovery
- provoke semantic instability
- force uncontrolled disclosure

ASD therefore treats observer interaction as adversarial semantic pressure applied against ProjectionWorld coherence.

---

## Core Assumption

An observer is not passive.

The observer continuously:
- forms hypotheses
- updates assumptions
- searches for inconsistencies
- evaluates plausibility
- tests environmental behavior
- measures response quality
- probes structural continuity

ASD must therefore maintain defensive semantic stability under active observation.

---

## Primary Threat Categories

### Semantic Consistency Probing

The observer attempts to identify contradictions inside ProjectionWorld.

Examples include:
- topology inconsistencies
- naming irregularities
- impossible relationships
- continuity failures
- unrealistic operational structure

Projection instability increases detection probability.

---

### Accelerated Discovery

The observer attempts to force rapid disclosure of:
- structures
- artifacts
- environmental logic
- hidden relationships
- operational assumptions

ASD counters this through:
- ExposureBudget
- adaptive pacing
- controlled ambiguity
- bounded disclosure

---

### Projection Drift

Projection Drift occurs when ProjectionWorld coherence degrades over time.

Possible causes include:
- uncontrolled adaptation
- excessive artifact generation
- semantic overexpansion
- observer-state reconstruction errors
- continuity mismatches

Projection Drift increases:
- suspicion
- instability
- observer confidence loss

---

### Overfitting To The Observer

ASD may become excessively adapted to a specific observer model.

This creates risks including:
- unrealistic precision
- artificial environmental behavior
- unstable adaptation loops
- reduced semantic plausibility

ProjectionWorld must remain globally believable, not merely locally optimized.

---

### Suspicion Escalation

The observer may progressively accumulate suspicion through:
- repeated anomalies
- timing inconsistencies
- excessive perfection
- unrealistic environmental behavior
- abrupt adaptation

Suspicion accumulation destabilizes ProjectionWorld.

ASD therefore intentionally preserves:
- ambiguity
- imperfection
- incomplete visibility
- environmental noise

---

### RealityBoundary Failure

A critical threat occurs if real operational structure leaks into ProjectionWorld.

This includes:
- infrastructure exposure
- privileged information leakage
- topology correlation
- operational metadata disclosure

RealityBoundary separation is mandatory.

---

## Defensive Stability Goals

ASD attempts to maintain:

- semantic continuity
- bounded adaptation
- observer uncertainty
- controlled discovery
- projection persistence
- explainable decision paths
- stable pacing
- defensive containment

---

## Safety Priority

SafetyBoundary constraints always override:
- strategic opportunity
- projection realism
- observer engagement quality
- adaptive optimization

Projection safety is considered more important than deception quality.

---

## Observer Variability

Different observers may:
- interpret artifacts differently
- tolerate inconsistencies differently
- escalate at different rates
- build hypotheses differently

ASD therefore avoids assuming:
- perfect observer modeling
- deterministic interpretation
- complete cognitive reconstruction

Observer-state estimation always contains uncertainty.

---

## Defensive Objective

The ASD threat model is not centered around defeating observers.

The objective is:
- defensive stabilization
- controlled interaction
- bounded semantic projection
- escalation delay
- defensive time acquisition
- explainable adaptive behavior

ASD attempts to maintain operationally safe semantic environments under adversarial observation pressure.
